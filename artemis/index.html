Flight milestones and engineering results
  • 21 Nov · The first powered lunar flyby put Orion on course for its distant orbit.
  • 25 Nov · Orion entered distant retrograde orbit.
  • 28 Nov · Orion reached its maximum distance from Earth.
  • 1 Dec · A departure burn put Orion on course for the second lunar flyby.
  • 5 Dec · The return powered flyby sent Orion back toward Earth.
  • 11 Dec · Orion used a skip entry and splashed down in the Pacific. Engineers later investigated unexpected heat-shield char loss and used the findings for crewed flights.

Planned sequence and test objectives
  1. Blue Origin sends a Blue Moon Mark 2 test vehicle to low Earth orbit.
  2. SLS launches the crew aboard Orion. The crew checks out Orion, raises its orbit, and approaches the waiting test vehicle.
  3. Orion docks with Blue Moon to practice operations needed during a crewed lunar mission.
  4. After Starship’s test vehicle launches, Orion undocks from Blue Moon and repeats rendezvous and docking with Starship.
  5. Orion returns to Earth. The mission also supports research into how lunar samples might be handled on future flights.

What NASA is evaluating
  • SpaceX is developing a Starship approach that refuels in Earth orbit. Blue Origin’s plan uses Earth-orbit transfer stages to send Blue Moon Mark 2 toward lunar orbit.
  • NASA is evaluating the current interim stage and a stretched version of Centaur V for SLS.
  • Axiom Space is developing lunar suits, including a lighter option intended for early surface sorties.

Flight hardware

SLS Block 1

322 ft (98 m) tall, about 5.75 million lb (2,608 t) fueled, up to 8.8 million lbf (39.1 MN) of thrust. Four RS-25 core engines and two solid boosters.

NASA SLS specifications

Orion

Designed for four crew and up to 21 days. The crew module returns to Earth. Its ESA-supplied service module provides power, propulsion, water, and air during flight.

Orion reference guide (PDF)

Commercial lunar landers

SpaceX’s Starship and Blue Origin’s Blue Moon Mark 2 are the two systems in NASA’s current lunar-lander development plans. Artemis III will test versions of both in Earth orbit.

NASA lander development update

Launch and recovery

Exploration Ground Systems prepares SLS and Orion at Kennedy Space Center, supports launches from Pad 39B, and works with recovery teams to retrieve Orion after splashdown.
